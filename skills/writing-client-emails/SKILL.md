# Writing Client Emails

Draft human, context-aware stakeholder correspondence without creating unapproved scope, promises, disclosures, or decisions. The deliverable is an email draft, not a sent message.

## Contracts and inputs

Read [COMMON.md](../../references/COMMON.md). Read [PROJECT_CONTEXT.md](../../references/PROJECT_CONTEXT.md) when using team identities, recipient mappings, or project commitments.

Inputs: email purpose, questions/items needing response, relevant facts, audience, and existing thread if replying. Use confirmed recipient names/roles and sender/signature only when supplied. Missing recipients can remain placeholders for a draft; never invent addresses, expand the recipient list, or impersonate a sender.

Ask targeted questions when missing context affects accuracy, sensitivity, scope, commitments, or the ability to reply. A provisional draft may flag gaps outside the body and use visible placeholders. Do not access mailboxes or fetch threads without authorization.

## Workflow

1. **Identify new email versus reply**. Read the supplied thread for context and unanswered questions. A reply should acknowledge the actual message, not pretend it is first contact.
2. **List items to address** before drafting. Separate factual updates, neutral clarification questions, proposals, agreed decisions, and requested actions.
3. **Establish evidence** for statements about scope, behavior, dates, status, approvals, or configurable values. Do not say “we have built,” “approved,” or “final” unless supported. Note facts/assumptions outside the draft where review is needed.
4. **Draft context** for each substantive question: enough plain-language explanation to answer without another meeting, why the answer matters, and only relevant background. Avoid unexplained jargon or internal artifacts the recipient cannot access.
5. **Ask one clear question per item**. Do not lead the recipient toward a preferred solution. Where alternatives are helpful, identify them as options/proposals, include an open alternative, and do not imply that a “yes” authorizes new scope automatically.
6. **Review scope and disclosure**: distinguish “what happens today?” from “what should we build?” Do not disclose unapproved internal details or confidential information. Proposals for new scope are permitted only when requested and explicitly labeled for separate assessment/approval.
7. **Review tone**: concise, courteous, natural, audience-appropriate. Trim corporate filler and excessive formatting; vary sentence lengths where useful. Formality and punctuation follow the user/client style, not claims that particular punctuation proves AI authorship.
8. **Deliver the draft** with unresolved placeholders/assumptions and any requested confirmation outside the sendable body. Do not send, schedule, or publish.

## Tone and scope guardrails

- Prefer a descriptive subject, brief context, clear asks, and an appropriate close. Avoid condescending lead-ins, defensive phrasing, unnecessary apologies, and artificial urgency.
- Match the existing relationship/thread and requested level of formality. Use greetings, thanks, or a sign-off when they serve the message; do not ban ordinary words such as “finalize” when they are accurate.
- Avoid repetitive setup/ask/close blocks where a shorter list works. Use bold sparingly, not on every phrase.
- Do not claim that a value is adjustable/configurable just to reassure the recipient. Both the capability and its scope need evidence. Proposed values remain proposed; do not invent thresholds or imply no cost/impact to change them.
- Do not refer to unseen designs as client-approved. When discussing an unreviewed option is authorized, label its status accurately.
- Dates and commitments require evidence or explicit proposal wording. Do not invent a response deadline or imply that no further input will ever be needed.
- Include only supplied/confirmed To/CC recipients. Relevant additional stakeholders may be suggested outside the draft, not silently added.

## Expected output

- Subject (or preserve supplied reply subject).
- To/CC only if known, otherwise omit or visibly mark placeholders.
- Sendable body with context, prioritized questions/updates, and a clear requested response.
- Review notes outside the body: unknown facts, scope/proposals, sensitive content to confirm, missing recipients/signature, and source basis where material.

Normal drafts remain chat-only. If a saved draft is requested, use the authorized communications/discovery destination under COMMON, read existing targets before update, check collisions and report exact saved paths. No separate file is needed just because an email was drafted.

## Examples

- **Neutral clarification:** “When a request lacks the required information, what does your team do next?” Do not replace it with “Would you like us to add an escalation dashboard?” unless a proposal is actually requested.
- **Reply:** supplied client says a suggested duration is too short. Acknowledge that feedback and ask which working pattern the duration needs to support. Do not promise configurability without evidence.
- **Insufficient input:** “Tell the client it is ready” with no readiness evidence. Ask what is ready and what supports that status, or provide a clearly provisional draft with the claim unresolved; do not invent completion.

## Final checks and limits

Check reply context, accurate recipients, plain-language context, one clear ask per item, scope neutrality, evidence for commitments, privacy, placeholders, and natural tone. Draft approval is not send approval. Sending would require separate authorization of the exact recipients, sender/account, content, attachments, and action, plus available capability and verification; it is not part of this drafting workflow.
